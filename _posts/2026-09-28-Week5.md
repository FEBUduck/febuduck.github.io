## A Different Kind of Statement Win
Friday , we wrote that the trip to Los Angeles would tell us a lot about this Oregon football team.

Not because USC was unbeatable.Not because one game would define the season. But because it would provide answers to many of the questions that had followed the Ducks since the loss.

Questions about quarterback play, coaching, the culture and leadership within the program.

Saturday night answered all three and it did so in a way nobody expected.

### The Quarterback Question

The spotlight entering the game was firmly on Dante Moore. 

Instead, the biggest storyline became Moore leaving the game in the second quarter after a dirty hit resulted in a concussion. Just like that, Oregon's biggest question mark became its biggest concern.

What happens if Dante can't play?

Fortunately for the Ducks, we got the answer. Raiola stepped in and never looked so good. The offense continued to move the football, the moment never appeared too big for him, and Oregon never looked like a team scrambling to survive without its starting quarterback.

In fact, the Ducks looked confident. If there was any doubt about Oregon's quarterback depth before Saturday, there shouldn't be now.

### The Coaching Question

While one game doesn't erase those concerns entirely, Oregon's performance against USC went a long way toward quieting them.

When Dante Moore went down in the second quarter, the entire outlook of the game changed. It would have been easy for the offense to tighten up, for players to press, or for the team to lose focus.

Instead, Oregon responded.

Raiola stepped in and the offense never missed a beat. The Ducks continued moving the ball, continued executing, and continued attacking a USC defense on the road in one of the biggest games of the season.

Just as importantly, Oregon kept its composure.

After the hit that knocked Moore out of the game, emotions were understandably high. Yet the Ducks didn't retaliate, didn't lose their discipline, and didn't hand USC free yardage through frustration penalties. They stayed focused on winning the football game. That's a credit to the coaching staff and Dan Lanning. Anyone can coach when everything is going according to plan. The true test comes when adversity strikes and emotions start running high. Against USC, Oregon passed that test.The Ducks lost their starting quarterback, kept their heads, trusted their backup, and walked out of Los Angeles with one of their biggest wins of the season.

### The Culture Question

More than anything else, Saturday may have answered questions about this team's culture.

Following the Oklahoma State loss, many fans wanted to see more accountability. More urgency. More fight, more evidence that this team genuinely cared about correcting its mistakes.

They got it.

The sideline stayed engaged. The team rallied around its backup quarterback.Players continued competing despite losing one of the most important players on the roster in a devestating fashion.
Instead of folding when things got difficult, Oregon seemed to get stronger. That's not the sign of a fractured locker room. That's the sign of a team that believes in itself.

### Looking Ahead

That doesn't mean every concern has disappeared.

The biggest question now is a simple one:

Can Oregon use the upcoming bye week to get healthy and maintain this momentum?

All eyes will be on Dante Moore and his recovery timeline. While everyone hopes to see him back as soon as possible, Saturday showed that Oregon is capable of surviving if he needs additional time.

The Ducks entered Los Angeles looking for answers.

They left with a 41-27 victory and a lot more confidence about who they are as a football team.

Now comes the challenge of carrying that momentum through the bye week and into the heart of the season.
