# One Night, Two Reputations

## Who Has More To Prove Against USC: Dante Moore or Dan Lanning?

When Oregon takes the field Saturday against USC, most of the attention is going to be on Dante Moore.
He's the quarterback. He's the face of the program. Oregon spent the entire offseason promoting him as a Heisman candidate and one of the biggest stars in college football.
But the more I think about it, the more I'm not sure he's the person with the most to prove this weekend.
That might actually be Dan Lanning.
### The Case for Dante Moore

No player on Oregon's roster faces more scrutiny than Dante Moore.
Some of that comes with the position. Some of it comes from the expectations Oregon helped create during the offseason.
Ever since arriving in Eugene, Moore has been viewed as the quarterback capable of leading Oregon to a national championship. The talent has never been in question.
What remains in question for some fans is everything else.
Does he have that intangible "it" factor that separates good quarterbacks from great ones?
Can he become the type of leader teammates rally around when adversity hits?
Can he consistently perform at an elite level against top competition?
Those questions only grew louder after Oklahoma State.
We’ll ignore all was he giving a tell talk, regardless some fans saw a quarterback who looked hesitant at times. Others felt he didn't always trust what he was seeing, leading to moments when he held onto the ball too long or appeared unsure of where to go with it. There have also been occasions this season where open receivers were available, yet Moore opted for a more difficult throw or failed to see the easier option altogether.
Whether those criticisms are fair is almost beside the point.
They exist.
And right now, USC presents the biggest opportunity for Moore to answer them.
Nobody is evaluating Dante based on what he did against Portland State. Oregon was always expected to dominate that game.
The games that shape a quarterback's reputation are the ones like this.
Prime time.
A ranked opponent.
A hostile environment.
A game where an entire season is on the line.
If Moore leads Oregon to a victory in Los Angeles, many of those lingering questions begin to fade. Not because one game solves everything, but because great quarterbacks are often remembered for how they perform when the spotlight is brightest.
Saturday night gives Dante Moore a chance to add one of those moments to his story.

### The Case for Dan Lanning

If we're being honest, I think the pressure is greater on Dan Lanning.
The Oklahoma State loss wasn't just about a missed throw, a blown coverage, or a bad bounce.
It was the first time in a long time that Oregon looked like the less prepared football team despite having the better roster.
That's a coaching issue.
Oregon can survive an occasional loss. Every program does. What fans struggle to accept are losses where a more talented team looks undisciplined, out-executed, and a step behind for most of the afternoon.
That's why so much of the conversation since Stillwater has centered on the coaching staff.
The offensive line is one example.
Three games into the season, Oregon still appears to be searching for its best combination up front. Competition is healthy, but eventually continuity matters too. Fans want to know who the best five are and why that answer still feels unsettled. More importantly, they want to see the development expected from one of the most talented offensive line rooms in the country.
The same concerns exist on defense.
Lanning arrived in Eugene with a reputation as one of college football's brightest defensive minds, yet Oregon's defensive identity still feels unclear at times. The linebacker room has often been criticized for inconsistent play and a lack of impact. Fair or not, when a defensive coach leads the program, fans naturally expect the defense to be the unit setting the standard.
And that's what makes this game so important for Lanning.
For the first time in his Oregon tenure, the conversation isn't about recruiting rankings, NIL success, or future potential.
It's about development.
It's about preparation.
It's about whether Oregon is becoming the program it has spent years trying to build.
A win over USC won't answer every question.
But if the Ducks look organized, physical, and wins on Saturday night, it becomes much easier to view Oklahoma State as a bad afternoon rather than a warning sign.
And right now, proving that may be the most important job Lanning has.

### So Who Has More To Prove?

For me, it's Lanning.
Not because I think Dante is off the hook.
Far from it.
Oregon needs its quarterback to play well if the Ducks are going to have a chance to leave Los Angeles with a win.
But quarterbacks win games.
Head coaches define seasons.
If Oregon goes into USC and looks sharp, disciplined, and prepared, most of the concerns from Oklahoma State start to fade into the background.
If they struggle again, the questions only get louder.
That's why Saturday feels so important.
We're not just evaluating a quarterback.
We're finding out whether Oklahoma State was a bad game, or whether it exposed problems that Oregon still hasn't fixed.
And that answer may tell us a lot more about Dan Lanning than it does Dante Moore.
